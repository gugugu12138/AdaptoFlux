Post-run clarification (frozen protocol and measurements remain unchanged)

Hardware name: Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-14650HX, verified using the read-only Windows
registry ProcessorNameString after the run. The frozen environment contains the
platform.processor fallback because its CIM lookup returned no processor name.
The host has 16 physical cores, 24 logical CPUs, and 51,257,880,576 bytes of RAM.
No CPU affinity was imposed. The one-second CPU utilization sample immediately
before formal measurement was 10.1%. Other ATF experimental timing/training was
coordinated to stop during this run, but this was not a dedicated isolated host.

Speedup is the median of five paired graph-level serial/parallel timing ratios.
It is not the ratio of independently aggregated serial and parallel medians.
Figure error bars span the minimum and maximum across the five graph-level
medians. They are not confidence intervals. No outlier or seed was excluded.

Independent audit confirmed all 30 formal cases, 1,260 raw timing measurements,
CSV/JSON medians, node/edge counts, pilot exclusion, and source/protocol/data
hashes. Maximum native-output error against the independent NumPy reference is
2.22e-16; serial and parallel outputs are exactly equal on all 30 graphs.

The 32x increase in operation count gives 33.18x higher serial median wall time
in this tested range. This supports an approximately proportional execution-cost
trend for this controlled workload, not a universal asymptotic performance claim.
Median 4-thread speedup is below 1 at all six sizes (0.610x to 0.811x). This is
consistent with Python scalar workload and scheduling/GIL overhead; the benchmark
does not isolate which overhead is responsible for each part of the difference.
